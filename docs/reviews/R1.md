## 3. Source checks

Method: the stored raw HTML of each filing was parsed independently with `lxml`
(`text_content()`, whitespace and curly quotes normalised) and searched for the first 12
and the last 12 words of the first and last extracted Item 1A paragraph.

| Filing | First paragraph (starts) | Last paragraph (starts / ends) | In original |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2021 | "ExxonMobil's financial and operating results are subject to a variety" | "Projections, estimates, and descriptions…" / "…discussed above and elsewhere in this report." | all four probes found, each head probe exactly once |
| FY2022 | same wording | same wording | all four probes found |
| FY2023 | same wording | same wording | all four probes found |

- Heading that follows Item 1A in all three filings: `ITEM 1B. UNRESOLVED STAFF COMMENTS`.
  The source text after the last paragraph reads Item 1B ("None") and then Item 2 in
  FY2021 and FY2022, and Item 1B then Item 1C (Cybersecurity) in FY2023.
- Chosen Item 1A heading: `ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS` at block index 76 of 1,197 (FY2021),
  76 of 1,190 (FY2022), 79 of 1,202 (FY2023). `find_candidates` returned exactly one
  Item 1A candidate per filing, so the table-of-contents rule had no competing candidate
  to reject here; the choice is the body heading only in the sense that it is the sole
  candidate, and it sits early in the document. I did not locate a separate table of
  contents in the source, so the block-index-versus-contents check was not performed
  beyond that. TOC behaviour is covered by the 1.4 tests (`tests/unit/parse/test_items.py`,
  `tests/integration/test_items_fixtures.py`), not duplicated here.
- Omitted tables or unreadable blocks: the Item 1A range holds 0 table blocks, 4 heading
  blocks and 40/38/39 text blocks per filing (counts from `extract_blocks` on each filing).
  The 4 headings are the section titles (for example "Supply and Demand"), kept as the
  paragraphs' heading path rather than as paragraphs. Not checked: whether any text in
  the original was dropped by the HTML extraction other than at the two ends above.
- The intro paragraph (¶1) has no heading path; the page labels it "(no heading)".

## 5. Observed baseline behaviour

Cases are listed in the order found, not by importance. "Page shows" is from the code
path and the rendered HTML structure; the word-level comparisons marked (offline) were
computed with `word_diff` outside the page, because the page shows a word diff only for
1×1 groups.

**A. A paragraph folded into another (FY2021 → FY2022, old ¶2–4 / new ¶2–3).**
Page shows: one replaced group, three old paragraphs against two new, with no statement
of which became which. Old ¶4 starts "COVID-19. The initial phase of the COVID-19
pandemic…". Source supports: the stand-alone COVID paragraph is absent in FY2022 (the
phrase is not in the FY2022 source); a single sentence about "the rate of recovery from
the COVID-19 pandemic" now ends new ¶3 (Economic conditions), which grew from 1,193 to
1,536 characters. A reader of the group sees nothing marking that sentence.

**B. A paragraph appended to another (FY2021 → FY2022, old ¶21–27 / new ¶20–25).**
Page shows: one group of seven old and six new paragraphs, no word diff. Source
supports: old ¶27 ("See also the discussion of "Supply and Demand,"…") exists in FY2022
as the last sentence of new ¶25, not as a paragraph. In the same group the heading
"Technology and low carbon solutions" (old ¶25) became "Technology and lower-emission
solutions" (new ¶24) (offline word diff); the page does not mark it.

**C. Near-identical paragraphs shown as whole red and green blocks (FY2021 → FY2022,
old ¶9–10 / new ¶8–9).** Page shows: four full paragraphs, two struck through and two
green. Source supports (offline): old ¶9 → new ¶8 differs by one word ("of" → "for");
old ¶10 → new ¶9 by deleting "certain of". The same effect: old ¶29 / new ¶27 ("on line"
→ "online"). The 1×1 groups in the same pair (for example old ¶19 / new ¶18,
"re-denominate" → "redenominate") are rendered with a word diff, so the same size of
edit is easy to see in one place and hidden in another depending on group shape.

**D. A new bullet inside a replaced group (FY2022 → FY2023, old ¶14–15 / new ¶14–16).**
Page shows: a replaced group of two old and three new paragraphs, with no "added"
marking. Source supports: new ¶16 starts "•regulatory interpretations that exclude or
disfavor our products" and has no counterpart in FY2022 (the phrase appears in no
FY2022 Item 1A paragraph, by substring search; the group's two old paragraphs resemble
new ¶14 and ¶15 at 0.85 and 0.98 character similarity, offline). The page has no green-only
"added" paragraph for it because it sits in a group. Both pairs have zero "added" ops.

**E. A deletion buried in a long paragraph (FY2022 → FY2023, old ¶2–6 / new ¶2–6).**
Page shows: a 5×5 replaced group. Source supports (offline word diff of old ¶3 / new
¶3): the clause "from the COVID-19 pandemic" is deleted, replaced by "public health
epidemics or pandemics", and "will" became "may"; the FY2023 paragraph has no COVID
mention. These sit inside a 1,500-character paragraph with no word-level marking.

**F. Moves.** No moved paragraph was observed: neither pair has an unmatched removed or
added paragraph. The moved-paragraph behaviour (removed in one place, added in another)
is exercised only by the unit test. I did not search for moved text other than through
the ops list.

**Handled well (controls).**
- FY2021 → FY2022, old ¶7 / new ¶6 (1×1): two inserted sentences appear as green
  insertions inside the otherwise unchanged paragraph; the source shows the same two
  sentences, the first beginning "In addition to direct potential impacts on our costs".
- FY2022 → FY2023, old ¶38 / new ¶39 (1×1, forward-looking-statement paragraph): the
  inserted item references "1C," and "5," are the only highlights; the source list reads

- FY2022 → FY2023, old ¶12 / new ¶12 (1×1): the added phrase "or punitive taxes on oil,
  gas and petrochemical operations" is the only highlight.
